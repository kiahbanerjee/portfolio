Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, yet few places carry more history, more art, or more cultural weight than the 44 hectares it occupies. This book, So Much Within So Little, is built entirely around that contradiction.

Rather than simply documenting the country, the book attempts to make that tension felt by developing a visual and structural system that reiterates the contrast between scale and significance without becoming repetitive or heavy-handed.

Vatican City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site dense with centuries of art, architecture, and sculpture like Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel, Bernini's colonnades, Raphael's frescoes - all contained within 44 hectares. This project reimagines its official identity through a modern and sleek lens, foregrounding that architectural and artistic legacy rather than its religious or political symbolism.

The book is structured to make the size contrast visceral. The tension between how little space Vatican City occupies and the sheer weight of what it holds. Content is organized around the city's key sites: St. Peter's Square, St. Peter's Basilica, the Sistine Chapel, and the Vatican Gardens.

Design

The book is structured to mirror the experience of moving through Vatican City - progressing from one site to the next the way a visitor would, building a sense of place and sequence as the reader moves through it.

The design is deliberately minimal, stepping back to let the architecture and art speak. A consistent system of large images paired with small, tightly spaced text runs throughout the book. The density of the type reflects the idea of so much packed into so little, while the scale of the images gives weight and presence to what is being shown. Images are cropped to isolate single details of large architectural forms, making them feel as though they bleed beyond the page itself, putting the size concept into context rather than simply stating it.

Between text sections, the book breaks into dedicated image sections presented in a gallery style, referencing the museum experience and giving the reader space to sit with the work. The cover takes a more collage-based approach, mirroring the image placement used throughout the Vatican Gardens section and setting up the logic of the book before it even opens. Every decision builds toward the same thing: a sequence of scale that accumulates as you read.

Inside the St. Peter's Basilica section, images of artworks and sculptures are reproduced at proportional scale to their real-life dimensions and sequenced in the order you would encounter them walking through the church. Rather than presenting them as isolated objects, this approach places the reader inside the space, giving the experience of moving through the Basilica and encountering each work the way it was meant to be seen.

The Sistine Chapel chapter moves through different sections of the ceiling, using color to isolate and highlight each part while unpacking its meaning. The layout is proportional to the real dimensions of the ceiling itself, and that measurement carries through the entire chapter, giving the reader a sense of the true scale of what Michelangelo painted.
