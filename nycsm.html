Archive CV

Street musicians are all around New York City, performing daily, putting in real work, and yet routinely dismissed as background noise or nuisance. Their stories rarely make it into publishing ecosystems or cultural memory, not because they lack significance, but because the platforms that document culture have never prioritized them.

This publication exists to change that. It features interviews with five artists, bringing their perspectives, experiences, and lives to the page and giving space to voices that are meaningful but rarely documented.

Detail

The publication features interviews with five street musicians, two of which were conducted by me. I asked about their background, their other sources of income, and what life actually looks like as a performing artist in this city. Each interview offers something different, and together they build a picture of a community that is far more complex than the city gives it credit for.

This is the first edition of what is meant to grow into an ongoing collection of stories from the musicians who soundtrack New York every day.

Design

The publication is bound with metal rings, referencing the structure of an album book and setting the tone for how it's organized. Rather than traditional chapter openers, sections are separated by header spreads listing the artist's information like name, genre, location and medium, treating each musician as an entry in a collection.

Different text layouts and size hierarchies distinguish between interview transcripts, historical context, and fun facts, giving each type of content its own visual voice. Each musician is also assigned a color, and all images are treated with a duotone effect to keep the book feeling cohesive despite the variety of angles, locations, subjects, and shooting styles across the photography.

Every artist section includes a dedicated spread that foregrounds a standout quote from their interview, with images arranged around it to reflect the feeling or theme of that moment, making each one feel less like a layout and more like a response.
