The internet is saturated with videos calling Indian street food dirty, disgusting, and unhygienic - a narrative built on the worst of a handful of vendors among tens of millions. For someone who grew up in India, this misrepresentation is very hard to watch. Street food is not a footnote to Indian culture; it is affordable, authentic, generous, and one of the most accessible ways to connect with a country and its people without stepping into a fine dining restaurant.

This book is a direct response to that narrative. It celebrates the vendors, the dishes, and a culture the internet has chosen to overlook.

Book Design / Creative Direction

2025

Detail

The book is organized by time of day and occasion, like, tea time snacks, after school bites, late night eats, ect. It follows the rhythm of everyday Indian life and the people who live it. Each section moves through the dishes most associated with that time and explores their significance and the role they play in daily culture.

Alongside the food, the book features portraits of the vendors who sustain it. It talks about the politics and economics of running a street food business, their battles within Indian society, and the realities of a livelihood the country depends on but rarely acknowledges.

Design

The design takes direct inspiration from the aesthetic of street vendor carts including bold lettering, slab serif typography, bright colors, floral motifs, and layered compositions. The form of the book is also intentional, the 11*17 inch landscape format mirrors the proportions of the carts themselves. The book is hardcover binded and using 170gsm paper gives it a substantial weight, reflecting the generosity of street food culture and the depth of history behind it.
