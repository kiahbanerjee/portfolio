Most reading experiences are designed for clarity - a beginning, a path, an end. This project asks what happens when you remove all of that, and build something that is infinite, disorienting, and impossible to retrace.

Creative Coding, Interaction Design

2026

The project takes its name and concept from Jorge Luis Borges' short story of the same name, which follows a man who acquires an impossible book - one with infinite pages and no beginning, no end, and no way to return to anything you have already read.

This premise became the foundation for the project. If a book could exist without sequence, without fixed structure, and without a clear path through it, what would that feel like as a reading experience? The site attempts to answer that question.

Mindmap for ideation

The project also draws from Project Xanadu, Ted Nelson's 1960 vision for a global system of permanently interconnected documents — one where text always points back to its original source, links never break, and all human knowledge exists as part of one living, referencing structure. It was an alternative vision for the web, built on the idea that everything is connected and nothing exists in isolation.

This idea of radical interconnection shaped the linking structure of the site, where internal and external links pull the reader between texts, ideas, and sources and reinforcing the sense that no piece of writing exists on its own.
